# ollopA: objects and user journeys (approved 15 September 2026)

*Written 14 September 2026 by the product lead, from memos 16, 17, 18 and 19 only. A fresh pass: the specs were not read, and the earlier journey list was set aside rather than edited, because it missed whole areas of the work — research, enrichment, AI-written content, real sequence building, calls, reply handling, the people who never open the app, and the marketer and CS days.*

**Citations.** `16§8` = memo 16 (Apollo workflows), section 8. `17§1` = memo 17 (sales-org inventory). `18§4` = memo 18 (AE and leader notes). `19§2` = memo 19 (RevOps and developer notes); `19 JD-3` is one of its six live RevOps postings.

**Boundary tags.** `[BOUNDARY: dialer | meetings | conversations | extension | API | MCP | CLI | forms]` marks a journey needing something PRODUCT.md puts outside the line or is silent on. Section 4 recommends what to do about each.

**The approval rule used throughout** (PLAN.md, 14 Sep 2026): the owner approves their agent's actions, the admin may approve for anyone; low-cost reversible actions — research, scoring, drafts saved not sent — are **logged, never queued**; per-item approval only for irreversible or costly ones — sending email, spending above a credit cap, changing a deal stage; a second, admin approval above a threshold that is itself a setting, default 1,000 recipients or 500 credits in one action; approvals batched at task boundaries with the consequence stated, never mid-task.

## 1. The objects

The test applied to each: would an SDR, AE, marketer, CSM or admin name it in a sentence about their day? "Notification" fails — people say "tell me in Slack when a reply lands", which is a setting on a signal, sequence or workflow, not something they keep, open, own or search. Nor is "view" or "widget" an object; a saved view is an attribute of a table.

The earlier list had about ten: contact, company, list, sequence, deal, campaign, account, task, report, user. The audits say that is half the model. Rows marked **[missed]** were absent and are load-bearing: without them, journeys below cannot be told at all.

**One object that is not on this list, and why.** A **comment** appears in L1 and L3 and in no row above. It is an attribute of a note — a note addressed to one teammate, which reaches them on Home and in the daily digest and never through the bell — not a thirty-second object. It is written on the deal, the call or the record it is about, and it lives in the same timeline as every other note.

**Four modelling decisions this forces.**

1. **Company and "account" are one object with a customer state.** The CSM reads goals, health and renewal on the same record the SDR reads firmographics and people on (17§5, 16§3). One template, two first-screen field sets chosen by the usage model.
2. **Renewals and expansions are deals, typed.** 38% of AEs now own them, nearly double 2024 (18§10), and CS forecasts its book (17§5). A renewal that is not a deal cannot be forecast.
3. **A call is not a task.** A connected disposition stops a contact advancing; not connected lets them continue (16§11). The task is the instruction, the call the outcome, and the outcome changes other objects.
4. **The request is an object, because the admin's inbox is the product's real queue.** Three of six RevOps postings name intake as a duty (19 JD-2, JD-3, JD-4), and behind it sit 11 hours a week per team of manual CRM work, 2.1 of it ad-hoc reporting, unbatchable because "report requests arrive daily" (19§4.1). A product with nowhere to put the request pushes it into Slack, where it cannot be counted.

## 2. The journeys

Fifty-four, by role: SDR 13, AE 7, marketer 6, CS 6, RevOps admin 10, sales leader 3, developer 4, agents as actor 5.

### SDR — 13

**S1 · Start the day from Home** — Meridian, daily: only here do three queues feed one list.
**Trigger** Sign-in. **Steps** Read overdue tasks, scan replies, scan agent items, choose the first block, work it, return at the boundary. **Objects** task, thread, agent item, sequence. **Decisions** Which block first; approve the agent batch now or after calls. **Approval** Ranking is agent-made and logged; drafts wait in a batch offered at the boundary, never mid-call. **Source** 16§1, 16§15; 17§1 (112 activities/day).

**S2 · Research an account and write the plan** — Fathom, weekly: nobody there has time to research.
**Trigger** An account tops the list, or a signal fires. **Steps** Read the company and its contacts, read the agent brief and its citations, check signals and recent activity, pick the personas, save a shortlist, write the angle, set the first task. **Objects** company, person, signal, score, note, agent run. **Decisions** Worth the touches; which two or three people; what the angle is. **Approval** Research is logged with citations, never queued — 17§9 makes "the agent ran" versus "the agent was right" the trust problem. **Source** 16§18, 16§2; 17§8, 17§9.

**S3 · Build a target-account list** — Meridian, weekly; Halyard does the same ten times.
**Trigger** New quarter, segment or signal; empty pipeline. **Steps** Filter by industry, size, geography and growth, layer a signal filter, exclude owned and in-sequence records, check the count against a week of touches, save the list, subscribe to alerts, find people by persona. **Objects** company, list, persona, signal, score. **Decisions** How wide; which exclusions; live filter or frozen. **Approval** Agents propose filters and lookalikes; applying is the rep's click, and nothing is spent until records are saved. **Source** 16§2; 17§1.

**S4 · Reveal emails and phones** — Fathom, daily: the credits are the founders' own money.
**Trigger** A list needs reachable contacts. **Steps** Select rows, choose email or email and phone, read the credit estimate, confirm, watch email status land, check the do-not-call flag, drop unusable rows. **Objects** person, credit, list, enrichment job. **Decisions** Phones or not, at several times the cost of an email; are catch-all addresses worth it. **Approval** The estimate and the per-user limit are the gates; above the admin threshold, the admin. **Source** 16 credit table (mobiles charged even for DNC numbers, no refunds); 16§3; 17§1.

**S5 · Import a CSV and run the waterfall** — Halyard, weekly: client lists arrive constantly.
**Trigger** A spreadsheet arrives, or saved records have holes. **Steps** Upload and map headers, set duplicate handling and owner, pick fields and confirm provider order, run ten rows, read hit rate and cost per hit, run the rest, read the report, discard what did not match. **Objects** person, company, enrichment job, credit, list, integration. **Decisions** Duplicate policy; provider order; stop at first verified or continue; what match rate is trustworthy. **Approval** Logged unless the run exceeds the per-run cap, when it becomes per-item with the credit figure stated. **Source** 16§3, 16§4; 17§1; 19§9 (one phone waterfall can reach 45+ credits).

**S6 · Re-prospect the job changers** — Ridgeline, monthly: a former user at a new employer is the best lead.
**Trigger** The job-change signal fires, or a bounce reveals a departure. **Steps** Confirm the move, update in place or create a new record, enrich the new address, check whether the new company is already an account, run a short warm sequence, stage the old record. **Objects** person, company, signal, enrichment job, sequence, credit. **Decisions** Update or duplicate; is the old employer a second opportunity. **Approval** The agent flags movers and drafts the opener: draft logged, send per-item. **Source** 16§4; 17§1, 17§8.

**S7 · Build a sequence, write it with AI, A/B a step** — Fathom, weekly: the founder writing it owns the message.
**Trigger** New segment or message, or a flat reply rate. **Steps** Create from scratch, template or clone; set schedule and ruleset; add steps and waits across email, call, LinkedIn and action items; write or generate each email from company context; preview on a real contact and check the variables resolve; activate; later add a variant to the weakest step, change one thing, wait for enough recipients, deactivate the loser. **Objects** sequence, step, template, snippet, field, report. **Decisions** Channel mix and length; link a template or clone it; which variable to test; when to call it. **Approval** Generation is a logged draft; promoting an A/B winner is the owner's click, because it changes what everyone sends. **Source** 16§8 (≥90% confidence, ~200 recipients), 16§9; 17§1.

**S8 · Enrol contacts and pick the mailbox** — Halyard, daily: many mailboxes per client, rotated.
**Trigger** A list is ready and a sequence active. **Steps** Select list and sequence, pick one mailbox or rotate several, see how many are net-new and the cost, check remaining daily capacity, skip unverified addresses, schedule, confirm day-one volume against the limit. **Objects** sequence, list, person, mailbox, credit. **Decisions** One mailbox or rotation; how many against capacity; send to unverified addresses at all. **Approval** Agent enrolment above 1,000 recipients carries the admin approval, stated as "N people from M mailboxes". **Source** 16§8, 16§10; 17§1 (41 emails/day implies several mailboxes per rep); 17§8.

**S9 · Work a call block and disposition every call** — Meridian, daily — `[BOUNDARY: dialer]`. The org's highest-volume work.
**Trigger** A calendar block, or call tasks coming due. **Steps** Filter tasks to calls sorted by score, read one line of context, dial, run the opener and take notes if connected, choose a purpose and a **disposition**, drop a voicemail or move on if not, let the disposition decide whether the contact advances, repeat for ninety minutes. **Objects** task, call, person, sequence, note. **Decisions** Who first; voicemail or not; which disposition, because it changes the sequence. **Approval** Queue order is agent-ranked and logged. No agent dials. **Source** 16§11; 16§23B ("call tasks and no way to complete them is a dead end"); 17§1, 17§10 #3.

**S10 · Complete a LinkedIn task** — Meridian, daily — `[BOUNDARY: extension]`. A fifth of the activity budget, outside the product.
**Trigger** A LinkedIn step comes due. **Steps** Read the message the step holds, copy it, go to LinkedIn, send the request or message, return, mark complete, check the weekly invite count against the cap. **Objects** task, step, person, snippet. **Decisions** Send as written or personalise; does the invite cap force a wait. **Approval** Copy may be agent-drafted (logged); the product never claims the action happened, only the rep marks it complete. **Source** 16§16 (never auto-completed, no connection-status column); 17§1 (19 touches/day, ~100 invites/week).

**S11 · Triage the reply inbox** — Meridian, daily: separated roles mean a reply often belongs to someone else.
**Trigger** Replies accumulate; several sweeps a day. **Steps** Open replies grouped by outcome, read, classify as interested, not now, objection, referral, wrong person, out of office or unsubscribe, act — book, snooze, rebut, reassign, suppress — update the stage, let the sequence finish, pause or continue. **Objects** thread, person, sequence, task, deal, meeting. **Decisions** The classification, because the action hangs off it; hand over now or after a qualifying call. **Approval** The agent pre-classifies and drafts per class: classification logged, send per-item with recipient and text shown. **Source** 16§10; 17§1 ("action mapping matters far more than a massive list of labels"); 17§10 #6.

**S12 · Book the meeting, chase the no-show, hand it over** — Meridian, daily — `[BOUNDARY: meetings]`. The SDR-to-AE seam exists only here.
**Trigger** A positive reply, or a call ending in agreement. **Steps** Offer times or a link; fill the handoff fields — the problem in the buyer's words, why now, who is involved, what success looks like; attach the recording; create the deal at stage one; assign the AE by territory; mark held or no-show; run the reminder and reschedule sequence on no-shows. **Objects** meeting, person, company, deal, note, task, sequence. **Decisions** Qualified against binary criteria; which AE; how hard to chase a no-show. **Approval** The brief is agent-drafted and logged; creating the deal the AE is measured on stays the owner's act. **Source** 16§12; 17§1 (no-shows 20–35%); 18§8a (binary criteria; rejection above 15% means misaligned standards; SDR pay depends on it).

**S13 · Rescue a sequence the bounce guard paused** — Fathom, monthly: one bad domain is the whole pipeline.
**Trigger** The bounce rate crosses the warn threshold, or the sequence auto-pauses. **Steps** Read the observed rate beside the threshold pair, find the mailbox or domain causing it, remove or re-verify bad addresses, check domain authentication, stop enrolling unverified addresses, lower volume, reactivate, watch for a day. **Objects** sequence, mailbox, domain, person, enrichment job. **Decisions** Clean the list or the domain; reactivate now or rebuild first. **Approval** The agent may propose the cleanup set (logged); changing the threshold pair is admin-only. **Source** 16§8 (4% warn, 6% pause); 16§10 (investigate above 5%); PLAN.md bounce guard.

### Account executive — 7

**A1 · Work the board and answer the warnings** — Meridian, daily: 214 open deals is a board, Fathom's nineteen a list.
**Trigger** Start of day, or an overnight warning. **Steps** Scan warnings — no activity, ghosted, overdue, too few contacts, no senior sponsor, stalled in stage; pick the three that matter; read each deal's last activity; edit amount, close date or stage inline; set the next step and its date. **Objects** deal, company, person, task, activity. **Decisions** Which warnings are noise; re-date or disqualify; what the real next step is. **Approval** Warnings are logged; an agent-proposed **stage change** is per-item, PLAN.md's irreversible class. **Source** 17§2; 18§1 (eight warning types; inline edit is now the dominant update path).

**A2 · Prepare, run and follow up a call** — Meridian, daily — `[BOUNDARY: meetings, conversations]`.
**Trigger** A meeting with external attendees. **Steps** Read who is coming and the brief — prior conversations, open objections, what was promised; check the qualification gaps; write the agenda; join with the recorder and consent notice; run the call; correct the summary and action items; generate the follow-up, edit, send inside one business day; turn action items into tasks with owners and dates. **Objects** meeting, conversation, deal, person, email, task, note. **Decisions** What to ask and what to skip; what to promise in writing; which actions are yours. **Approval** Brief, summary and tasks are logged; **the send is per-item** — the one gate every vendor ships. **Source** 16§12, 16§13; 18§3; 18§1 (follow-up inside a day cuts deal duration 11%); 17§9.

**A3 · Fill and validate the qualification fields** — Meridian, weekly per deal: MEDDPICC is in four of five 2026 AE postings.
**Trigger** A stage gate blocks the advance, or the review is tomorrow. **Steps** Read what the agent extracted with its evidence; validate, correct or delete; fill gaps by hand; map the economic buyer, champion and blockers; note who is actually engaged; mark validated; advance if the exit criteria are met. **Objects** deal, person and roles, conversation, field, note. **Decisions** Is the evidence good enough to call a champion a champion; is the economic buyer identified or assumed. **Approval** Extraction is logged with source quotes and a visible state — suggested, edited, validated — and never silently overwrites a validated value. **Source** 18§2 (Model A overwrites with no review step; Model B ends "validated by the AE or manager"); 18§5.

**A4 · Keep next steps and close dates honest** — Meridian, weekly: 17% of the rep week is manual data entry.
**Trigger** The weekly review, or a close date going past. **Steps** Filter to deals with no next step, a past close date, or nothing moved in fourteen days; decide each — still real, re-date, archive; write a next step that means something, with a date; correct the amount; set the forecast category; leave one line for the manager. **Objects** deal, task, note, report. **Decisions** Honest re-dating versus wishful; when to archive a lost deal, since ollopA has no closed-lost stage. **Approval** The agent proposes next steps from the last conversation; accepting is a click per deal, and auto-accept is deliberately not offered. **Source** 18§1 ("fastest closers discuss next steps 53% more"); 17§9; PLAN.md deal stages.

**A5 · Submit the weekly forecast** — Meridian, weekly: a forecast hierarchy needs more than one level.
**Trigger** The submission reminder. **Steps** Read the roll-up of commit, best case and pipeline; drill into the biggest and riskiest; adjust a category where the board disagrees with you; enter the number; write the judgement note; check the change history; submit. **Objects** deal, report, user hierarchy, note. **Decisions** What counts as commit — roughly a 90% number; what to omit; how to explain a drop. **Approval** A predicted number sits beside the rep's with the deals driving the difference; the agent never submits. **Source** 18§4 (Pipeline / Best Case / Commit / Omitted / Closed; commit ≈ 90%); 19§4.4.

**A6 · Prepare for and survive the deal review** — Meridian, weekly: a manager with eight AEs is a Meridian shape.
**Trigger** The standing review or 1:1. **Steps** Filter to the deals the manager flagged; confirm each has an owner, close date and next step, or fix it first; bring the one deal that most needs help; answer the manager's comments in place; agree actions and owners; set the tasks before leaving the call. **Objects** deal, task, comment, report. **Decisions** Which deal to put up; what help to ask for; what to concede is dead. **Approval** Agent risk lists are logged; nothing is approved here — the product supports a conversation rather than automating it. **Source** 18§5 (async comments; Clari's 90/10 ratio; no owner, date and next step means no review); 19§7.

**A7 · Close won and hand off to CS** — Meridian, monthly: the only business with both an AE and a CSM.
**Trigger** The deal is marked won. **Steps** Fill the handoff — goals in the customer's words, signer versus users, promises beyond the order form, risks and dissenters, the deadline and why; attach recordings and the proposal; mark the handoff complete, the gate on closed-won; hold the internal call; send the warm introduction; confirm the kickoff; transfer ownership. **Objects** deal, company, person, brief, conversation, task, meeting, user. **Decisions** What to declare a risk; which commitments were really made; when to let go. **Approval** The brief is agent-drafted and editable; the AE signs it, and the CSM sees who wrote which part. **Source** 17§2 ("Handoff Complete" required before Closed Won); 18§8b (doc day 2, internal call day 4, intro day 5, kickoff two weeks).

### Marketer — 6

**M1 · Build an audience and apply the suppressions** — Ridgeline, weekly: lifecycle sends punish a suppression miss.
**Trigger** A brief lands, or a standing audience needs refreshing. **Steps** Define rules by lifecycle stage, usage, industry and persona; choose live or frozen; suppress customers, open deals, recent closed-lost and anyone in an active sequence; check size and overlap; preview a sample; attach to the campaign; set the refresh cadence. **Objects** audience, person, company, deal, campaign, persona. **Decisions** Which suppressions are non-negotiable; live or frozen; is the size worth the build. **Approval** An agent may propose rules from a plain-language brief; the rules stay visible and editable before anything attaches. **Source** 17§4 ("suppression checks a non-negotiable part of campaign QA"); 16§3.

**M2 · Build, QA, launch and follow up a campaign** — Ridgeline, weekly: campaigns are the revenue motion here.
**Trigger** The brief is approved and the date set. **Steps** Attach the audience; write or generate the copy; set sender name, address and preheader; preview on desktop and phone; have someone who did not build it run the QA checklist; sample-send; schedule; watch the first hour; for events, split the follow-up by attended, no-show and hand-raiser and pass hand-raisers to sales; report at seven days. **Objects** campaign, audience, template, email, sequence, report. **Decisions** Send time; does a QA failure block the launch; what counts as a hand-raiser. **Approval** Copy is a logged draft; **the launch is per-item and crosses the admin threshold** whenever the audience exceeds 1,000, which it almost always does. **Source** 17§4 (SLAs 5/10/15 days; "a builder should not QA their own work"; 33% attendance, attended-to-pipeline 11.2%); PLAN.md threshold.

**M3 · Maintain the lead-scoring model** — Meridian, monthly: scoring matters where marketing hands to a separate team.
**Trigger** Sales complains about quality, or the quarterly recalibration. **Steps** Pull the last 50–100 closed-won customers, read the score each carried at first sales contact, take the median and reset the threshold, rebalance fit against behaviour, check decay still fires, preview the distribution, publish and tell sales. **Objects** score, person, company, deal, persona, report. **Decisions** Where the threshold sits; which behaviours to reward; grandfather those already routed or not. **Approval** Scoring runs unsupervised and logged; moving the threshold is a human publish, because it changes who gets called. **Source** 17§4 (the median method; threshold 40–80; "if 80% of your database scores above MQL, the threshold isn't doing work"); 16§6.

**M4 · Route new MQLs and honour the SLA** — Meridian, daily: the seam exists only where the teams are separate.
**Trigger** A form fill, a score crossing the threshold, an intent surge. **Steps** Match the lead to an account, apply the named-account override, apply the score gate, assign by weighted round robin skipping anyone on leave, notify the rep, start the SLA timer, reassign anything past the window, review misroutes weekly. **Objects** person, company, score, user, territory, workflow, task. **Decisions** Round robin or named ownership; the window by lead heat; what happens to sub-threshold leads. **Approval** Routing executes unsupervised and logged; the exception queue is where the human works. **Source** 17§4 (2–4 hours for high intent; average response 47 hours; sub-five-minute leads close 32% versus 12%); 18§8a.

**M5 · Work inbound form submissions** — Ridgeline, daily — `[BOUNDARY: forms]`. Inbound is the front door here.
**Trigger** Someone submits a form. **Steps** The submission creates or matches a person, enrichment fills firmographics inside the credit cap, the lifecycle stage is set, routing runs, the contact reaches the rep's queue with the answers attached, the rep reads them before the first touch, the conversion reports back to the campaign. **Objects** person, company, enrichment job, credit, campaign, score, workflow. **Decisions** Which fields to ask versus enrich; the cap; hide fields already known or not. **Approval** Enrichment on submission runs inside a cap, logged; no send without the rep. **Source** 16§5 (form builder, "Limit credit usage", automate follow-up); 16§23A.

**M6 · Report on campaigns and reallocate** — Ridgeline, monthly: where lifecycle is the engine, this is the consequential act.
**Trigger** Month end, or a channel that visibly stopped working. **Steps** Compare sourced against influenced pipeline, read conversion by audience and persona rather than channel alone, find the segment carrying the result, read unsubscribe and complaint rates alongside, decide what to stop, brief the next round, export for the leadership pack. **Objects** report, campaign, audience, deal, persona. **Decisions** Sourced or influenced as the currency; what to kill; where next month goes. **Approval** Summaries and anomaly callouts are logged; nothing is spent or sent. **Source** 17§4; 16§10; 17§11 (sourced versus influenced is a convention, not a benchmark).

### Customer success — 6

**C1 · Receive the closed-won handoff** — Meridian, monthly: the only business with a real AE-to-CSM seam.
**Trigger** A deal is won and the handoff record appears. **Steps** Read it — goals in the customer's words, signer versus users, promises beyond the order form, risks, the deadline and why; read the logged calls on the deal — purpose, disposition, duration, notes — and the transcript where an integration supplied one; list what is missing and ask the AE in the record, not by email; confirm terms, seats and renewal date; set the health baseline; book the kickoff. **Objects** company, deal, brief, call, person, task, meeting, score. **Decisions** Is the handoff complete enough to accept; what risk to flag on day one. **Approval** The brief is agent-assembled and logged, with the AE's own words marked apart from the generated summary. **Source** 17§5; 18§8b ("the step most orgs skip" — custom commitments); 16§13.

**C2 · Run onboarding to first value** — Ridgeline, weekly: the revenue model depends on value without a human driving the clicks.
**Trigger** The kickoff is booked. **Steps** Hold the kickoff and agree in writing what first value means, send the recap that fixes that definition, run setup and integration, train by role, confirm the milestone in production, widen adoption past the champion, set the next check-in. **Objects** company, person, task, meeting, note, score. **Decisions** The binary exit criterion at each stage; when an account is slipping. **Approval** The agent tracks usage against the milestone and raises a flag; escalating is the CSM's call. **Source** 17§5 (seven stages, one binary exit criterion each; "past 90 days without a first value milestone, treat the account as a churn-risk signal").

**C3 · Watch health and run a risk play** — Ridgeline, daily: continuous usage data here, no CS role at all at Fathom.
**Trigger** A score turns, or a risk flag is raised. **Steps** Sort accounts by health, read what moved and which input caused it, check logins, feature spread and tickets, decide the tier of risk, run the matching play, log it and the outcome, set the recheck date, tune the input if the flag was wrong. **Objects** company, score, signal, task, person, note. **Decisions** Real risk or noisy input; who owns the save; escalate to a sponsor or not. **Approval** Recalculation and sentiment flagging run unsupervised and logged; contacting the customer is human, or an approved send. **Source** 17§5 (eight scorecards, each with a named owner; "a health score three weeks old is a history report").

**C4 · Run the business review and work the renewal from T-120** — Ridgeline, monthly: 74% of SaaS revenue now comes from existing customers.
**Trigger** The account's quarter, or the renewal date entering the window. **Steps** Pull health, usage and goal progress; assemble summary, KPIs and ROI against the goals agreed at kickoff; run it in under an hour; capture actions with owners and dates; then T-120 surface the risk, T-90 align internally, T-60 run the value review and open the commercial conversation, T-30 finalise terms and confirm expansion. **Objects** company, deal (renewal), report, score, meeting, task. **Decisions** Which outcomes to claim; who owns the commercial conversation, CS or sales; bundle an expansion or not. **Approval** Data assembly and the draft narrative are logged; the claims are the CSM's to approve, and price is never agent-decided. **Source** 17§5 (seven-section deck; the T-120/90/60/30 ladder; CS owns health and ROI, sales owns contract and pricing); 18§10.

**C5 · Act on an expansion signal and route it** — Ridgeline, weekly: seat utilisation crossing a threshold is the canonical trigger.
**Trigger** Seats near the limit, a usage spike, a new executive, a promoted champion. **Steps** The signal queues a task, check relationship quality and history, write the one-page brief — what fired, who to talk to, their priorities, the timing; route by size band, small to CS and larger to an AE; hand over; track whether the conversation happened inside the SLA; record the outcome so the threshold can be tuned. **Objects** signal, company, person, task, brief, deal, user. **Decisions** Is the signal real; who runs the conversation; how fast. **Approval** Detection, drafting and routing proposals are logged; creating the expansion deal is human or per-item. **Source** 17§5 (under $15K to CS, $25K+ AE-led; champion move → 5 business days; new-user adoption → 48 hours).

**C6 · Forecast the renewal book** — Ridgeline, monthly: where renewals are the revenue, this is a real number.
**Trigger** The forecast cycle. **Steps** List every renewal in the period, assign probability from health, engagement and commercial state, separate at-risk from likely, keep expansion upside apart from the base, roll up, write the judgement note, submit, compare last cycle's calls against what happened. **Objects** deal, company, score, report, note. **Decisions** What health justifies a commit; forecast expansion at all or not. **Approval** A predicted probability sits beside the CSM's number with its inputs shown; the submitted number is human. **Source** 17§5 ("own renewal forecasting for assigned book of business"); 18§5 ("Protect the Base").

### RevOps admin — 10

**O1 · Set the workspace up, and let it correct itself two weeks later** — Fathom, once: set-up is a founder's ten minutes.

**O3 · Connect the CRM and configure the sync** — Meridian, once then monthly: Fathom skips it entirely.

**O4 · Clear the sync errors, and the data rules that caused them** — Meridian, weekly: Halyard's version is ten smaller logs.
**Trigger** The error count grows, a record never arrived, or the hygiene sweep is due. **Steps** Group errors by type — duplicates, permissions, picklists, mappings, bad values, validation failures; fix the biggest group; bulk-retry it; confirm the count falls; then check email, phone, job title, company name and last enrichment date, flag records missing two or more or older than twelve months, queue them for enrichment; record duplication, completeness and bounce rate against target. **Objects** integration, field, person, company, enrichment job, report. **Decisions** Fix here or in the CRM; retry or let the record go; is the validation rule worth its cost. **Approval** Root-cause suggestions are logged; retries write to another system, so they are the admin's click. **Source** 19§2.6 (nine error types, resync 100 at a time; the notification-frequency setting *is* the review cadence); 19§3.4 ("areas 2 and 3 are one feedback loop"); 19§3.6 (the five-field audit; duplication under 3%).

**O5 · Set permissions and territories, and answer an upgrade request** — Meridian, monthly: at Fathom everyone is an admin.
**Trigger** A role changes, a region is carved, or a teammate asks for a locked feature. **Steps** Prefer a small additive grant over a profile per person; assign; preview with view-as and confirm gaps explain themselves rather than appearing as blanks; adjust the territory filters; open the upgrade requests queue; read what the person was trying to do, the cost and the plan; approve, or decline with a reason. **Objects** profile, user, territory, plan, request. **Decisions** Least privilege versus friction; is the request worth the plan step. **Approval** None. PLAN.md forbids gating safety or decision-critical items, and forbids gating mid-task. **Source** 16§22; PLAN.md gating and the three kinds of "cannot see it"; 19§1.2; 19§1.4 (a set **overrides** individual permissions; an admin may only grant what they hold).

**O6 · Manage credits: caps, burn and attribution** — Halyard, weekly: credits are per workspace, so the agency reviews ten.
**Trigger** A burn spike, a balance alert, or the monthly review. **Steps** Read burn against balance and days remaining; break it down by feature, person and **surface** — app, automation, API, MCP, CLI, agents; find the job or person driving it; set a per-user limit and a team budget; cap the agent's per-run spend; set a spike alert; reorder providers; hand back unused seats. **Objects** credit, user, seat, agent, enrichment job, token, plan. **Decisions** Cap the person or the job; buy more or spend less; which surface to throttle. **Approval** Actions above the credit threshold already needed the admin; this is where the threshold itself is checked. **Source** 16§22, 16§17; 17§6 ("daily anxiety"); 19§6.5 (**credit budgets, spike alerts, MCP usage per user** — shipped in 2026 after the failure mode appeared); 19§6.1.

**O7 · Set the sending rules once, for everyone** — Halyard, weekly per client: Meridian sets these once a year.
**Trigger** A new client workspace, a new domain, or a deliverability scare. **Steps** Set the schedule and time zones; per-mailbox daily and hourly limits and the minimum delay; the bounce-guard pair, warn 4% and pause 6%, which every page then reads; the ruleset — what finishes, pauses or excludes a contact; the unsubscribe text and whether users may disable it; sequence priority across mailboxes; tell the reps. **Objects** mailbox, domain, sequence, workspace, stage. **Decisions** How much volume the domain carries; may reps override; the exact threshold pair. **Approval** None; the thresholds are decision-critical and always visible under rule 7. **Source** 16§8; 16§10 (50–100/day, 10–20/hour); PLAN.md bounce guard; 17§8 (fifteen minutes per client per week).

**O8 · Keep the workspace compliant** — Meridian, monthly: Meridian sells into Europe, Fathom would not know to look.
**Trigger** A regulation, an opt-out, or the review date. **Steps** Restrict who may not be prospected, emailed or tracked by region; turn on do-not-call screening and re-synchronise the list often enough to stay legal; review and export the rolling removal list; delete matching records here, in the CRM and from any job that would re-import them; confirm nothing on it feeds a sequence, an enrichment run, a key or an agent; record the review. **Objects** person, workspace, list, enrichment job, integration, key, agent. **Decisions** Which regions; is there a legal basis to keep a record; how to prove the deletion later. **Approval** Agents inherit the restrictions and may never act on a restricted record; the restriction is stated on the item, not silently filtered. **Source** 16§22, 16§11; 19§3.7 (synchronise "at least every 31 days", records kept 24 months; GDPR Art. 5(1)(d)).

**O9 · Take in a change request, make the change, check it landed** — Meridian, monthly: four teams ask at once here.
**Trigger** Somebody asks for a new field, stage, routing rule or report. **Steps** Capture the request before estimating it — what should someone be able to do afterwards, and why; name the decision owner; separate approval to investigate from approval to implement; build and test somewhere safe; roll out in small batches; tell the affected people in the flow of their work; watch whether they do it differently; record the decision and the rollback path. **Objects** request, field, stage, workflow, profile, report. **Decisions** Real gap or preference; who owns the decision; how to undo it. **Approval** Drafting the change and its documentation is agent work, logged; nothing that alters what others see ships without the decision owner. **Source** 19§8.2 ("separate approval to investigate from approval to implement"); 19§8.3; 19§8.4 ("attendance… does not tell you who can complete the work"); 19 JD-3.

**O10 · Meet a locked feature in the middle of the work** — Fathom, monthly: on Starter, gating is felt constantly.
**Trigger** Someone reaches a control their plan does not carry — a second mailbox, a shared ruleset, an export. **Steps** The control sits where it would live, with a lock and the plan name; clicking opens a panel — what it does, which plan carries it, the monthly cost, one button; an admin upgrades there; a non-admin sends an upgrade request from the same panel with the task attached; the work continues by another route either way; when it unlocks, nothing moves. **Objects** plan, workspace, user, request. **Decisions** Upgrade, ask, or route around. **Approval** Agent count is itself plan-gated: two on Starter, three on Growth, all plus a model key on Scale. **Source** PLAN.md gating; 16§8 (saving reusable rulesets is gated while configuring one sequence is not — the pattern to copy).

### Sales leader — 3

**L1 · Inspect the pipeline before the 1:1s** — Meridian, weekly: a leader with a team exists only here.
**Trigger** The standing weekly review. **Steps** Check the team's deal count and total look right; read the warnings; look for two-way activity, not just rep activity; check coverage against what is left to sell, early in the quarter; open flagged deals and see who is engaged by title; comment on the deals rather than saving it for the meeting; filter to one rep and repeat. **Objects** deal, company, person, comment, report. **Decisions** Which deals to challenge; which rep needs help rather than pressure; is coverage adequate for this win rate. **Approval** Risk scoring and warnings are logged; the comments are human. **Source** 18§5; 18§9; 19§4.5 (**required coverage = 1 ÷ win rate**, so 20% needs 5x — "the 3x benchmark is a starting point, not a standard").

No more chasing"); 19§4.3 ("the call is for decisions, not for data gathering"); 19§7.

**L3 · Coach from the calls** — Meridian, weekly — `[BOUNDARY: conversations, re-scoped]`. 46% of reps say they rarely get feedback.

## 4. Boundary decisions for the owner

One line each with a recommendation. Thirty-three per cent of Apollo's 234 documented workflows sit wholly or partly outside the current line (16§23), so this is not a rounding error. Eleven rows: the ten areas asked for, plus webhooks, which memo 19 promotes into a decision of its own.

| Area | Recommendation | Why, from the evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Dialer** | **Come inside minimally: log calls with purpose, disposition, duration and notes. Build no telephony — the rep dials however they dial and records the outcome here.** | A connected disposition stops a contact advancing; not connected lets them continue (16§11). Outcomes drive stage triggers and analytics. 44 dials a day is the SDR's largest single activity (17§1). "An SDR with call tasks and no way to complete them is a dead end" (16§23B). |
| **Meetings** | **Keep the booking page out; bring the meeting object and the booked, held and no-show events in.** | "Books meetings" is the SDR's job in PRODUCT.md, and meeting-booked is a sequence finish condition, a stage trigger and a workflow trigger (16§12, 16§17). No-shows run 20–35%, a workflow of their own (17§1). Without the event, sequences, stages and agents lose a trigger. |
| **Conversations** | **Keep recording and transcription out; bring in the two pieces that write to objects ollopA owns — the follow-up draft and the extracted-field proposal with its source quote.** | Recording, trackers, scorecards and coaching are a whole product (16§13), but both of those land on ollopA records (16§23B), and A3 is unrunnable without the second. Accept transcripts as an integration input rather than producing them. |
| **Extension** | **Stay out of the browser, but stop pretending LinkedIn work does not exist: the task, its message text, its copy button and its manual completion live inside ollopA.** | 19 touches a day is a fifth of the activity budget, capped at ~100 invites a week (17§1). Apollo never auto-completes a LinkedIn task and has no connection-status column (16§16), so honest manual completion is the whole feature and needs no extension. |
| **API** | **Inside, and said out loud: define what keys reach, publish the limits, make spend attributable per key.** | Keys are in the settings inventory while endpoints are undefined (16§23C). Nightly enrichment, reverse ETL and no-code automation are documented weekly work (17§7, 19§14). Copy 19§9: limits **per team, not per key**, or the workaround is minting keys; reads free while enrichment charges — publish the tail, since one waterfall phone reaches 45+ credits. No API on Starter, as decided. |
| **Webhooks** | **Inside, with a written delivery contract: at-least-once, signed, attempt-numbered, retried for 24 hours, never silently disabled — plus a reconciliation endpoint that makes the sweep cheap.** | 19§10 shows the field varies by an order of magnitude and nobody documents it in one place: no retry on a 5xx with a 5-second timeout; a 45-second total window; a subscription that disables itself permanently after 24 hours. ollopA can be the one that states its contract. Apollo documents no inbound subscription at all (16§23), so nothing conflicts. |
| **MCP** | **Inside as a first-class surface, with a three-tier scope — read, safe writes, destructive writes — and make it the case that tests the approval rule where there is no screen.** | Thirteen GTM vendors shipped one in 2025–26 (19§11): table stakes, not a bet. "Your AI tool becomes the workspace where GTM gets done" challenges a product whose thesis is what a screen shows first (16§23C). Copy Close's scope header, HubSpot's rule that scope is the intersection of installed tools and granted permissions, and Clay's per-user MCP spend attribution. Per-user, not admin-level; same permissions, credit balance and log. |
| **CLI** | **Admit it exists, scope it narrowly: auth including a device flow, search, export, bulk update with resume, allocation check, analytics.** | A real CLI ships with OAuth and no API key across records, deals, sequences, calls, tasks and analytics (16§21). 19§12 gives the shape: every bulk operation has a resume variant, and the admin namespace checks the allocation before a run. For Halyard's ten workspaces it is ten clicks versus one loop. |
| **Forms** | **Bring the form in as an object, with enrichment on submission and a hard credit cap. Do not build a page builder.** | Campaigns and audiences are inside already, and a form is how an audience is built from inbound (16§23A). Form enrichment is credit-free in the source and cheap to model; the cap is the interesting control (16§5). This belongs to campaigns, not to "website builder". |
| **Website visitors** | **Out, with one exception: accept "visited the site" as an inbound signal from an integration.** | It is analytics rather than a website builder (16§23), but identification is US-only for people, takes up to seven days to settle, and needs a snippet on the customer's site — all cost, little disclosure interest. Take the signal, not the surface. |
| **Signals and scoring** | **Both fully inside. They are the missing configuration for agents that already "score leads".** | PRODUCT.md's agents score leads and the settings inventory has no scoring model at all (16§23A). Scores decide routing (17§4), health (17§5), task order (16§15) and expansion triggers (17§5), and Ridgeline's whole shape is signal-driven. Without a score object the Agents page has no reason to show. |

**What stays out, plainly.** Paid ads, a website builder, a support desk, and billing the customer's own customers. The sweep confirms Apollo does none of them either (16§23); nothing in the evidence pulls the line that way.

**One consequence to name before the specs move.** If MCP and the CLI come inside, progressive disclosure has to say something about surfaces with no screen to disclose on: the approval batch, the consequence line and the credit cap must arrive in a terminal or a chat client and still be readable. Memo 19 sharpens it — the MCP pattern actually shipping inverts the org chart, with ops encoding the rules once and reps consuming them elsewhere (19§11), which moves the question from "what does this screen show first" to "what does this scope allow at all". The second question has a much shorter answer, and the library has no case for it yet.
