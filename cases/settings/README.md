# Case: Settings

*Built from [specs/14-settings.md](../../specs/14-settings.md), sections 5 and 7. The common version is a parody of Apollo's settings as documented in [knowledge-base/sources/07-apollo-settings-map.md](../../knowledge-base/sources/07-apollo-settings-map.md); every problem it shows names its section in that memo, and nothing in it is invented. Open the lesson at `#/learn/settings`.*

## The task

Meridian Software's RevOps admin, Daniel Okafor, is here most weeks. Somebody joined or left, a mailbox's bounce rate moved, the CRM sync threw errors overnight, an agent is waiting for approval on a 2,220-person enrolment, and a teammate has asked for a feature the plan does not include. Forty-two seats on the Scale plan at $5,418 a month, 1.84M credits left of 2.5M, burning 410k a week. The marketer comes for the scoring threshold and the personas; the SDRs come for their own mailboxes; the founder at Fathom comes for the credit balance every day because there is not much of it. The one thing nobody on this page may lose sight of is **what the workspace costs and what can spend or stop it on its own** — the price and renewal, the credit burn and its run-out date, the bounce guard that pauses sending, the do-not-call clock, what the agents may do without a human, and the two actions that end the workspace.

## The common version

Settings stops being a page and becomes a product: the main navigation is replaced by a 300-pixel sidebar of its own, and the page you land on is not a setting but an onboarding checklist with four progress rings. A hundred settings are dealt into fourteen pages under eight collapsible groups with names like Rules of engagement, Credits and activity and Objects, fields, stages; sub-pages add a row of horizontal tabs, a mailbox adds four more tabs inside a drawer, and reaching a signature is six interactions. The price is only on the Manage subscription checkout, Cancel plan is at the foot of Plan overview behind a reason picker, and there is no delete at all — the help centre says to contact Support. It is not a bad team. It is what happens when every request is added where it fits and nothing is ever moved: the structure records the order the features arrived in, not the order anybody needs them.

### Notes that are not steps

**Rule 6.** Nothing on this page moves because of what somebody did last week. The layout is read from the usage model for the seat and the business — a segment-level decision taken in the spec, not an inference about the individual. The only things that change on their own are values driven by object state: the bounce rate, which mailboxes are paused, the sync error count, the credit balance. The one thing that appears on a signal, the two-week exposure, adds a page temporarily, asks before keeping it, moves nothing else, and shows its own signal count on this page. Evidence: Findlater and McGrenere (2004), static menus significantly faster than adaptive; Todi et al. (CHI 2021) and Gaspar-Figueiredo et al. (*JSS* 2025), spatial adaptation loses at any accuracy (`11-what-changed-2018-2026.md`).

**Rule 3.** The common version has no Basic or Expert mode and neither does the product, so there is nothing to move. Its "Advanced sync" and "Advanced Intent Settings" blocks were audience labels rather than modes, and step 4 renamed them by content. Evidence: Cooper on perpetual intermediates; Nielsen 2026, the expert "is a regular user having a rare moment" (`09-expert-voices.md`).

## The numbers

Every number is `src/ollopa/usage/settings.ts`, 102 items, one per setting, with the method in [USAGE-MODEL.md](../../USAGE-MODEL.md). They are illustrative, fitted to the published shape — Nielsen's 80/20, Pendo's concentration benchmark, McGrenere and Moore's 27%-of-functions measurement — and every item carries its own reasoning in a `note`. The page reads them through `levelOf()`; it holds no list of what goes where. Thirty-five of the 102 are level one for Meridian's admin: nineteen because they are used in at least 20% of weeks, sixteen because they are decision-critical and rule 7 puts them there whatever the number.

## Deviations from the spec, stated plainly

- **The chevron on a door is the `Door` primitive's and is there at every step**, including the steps before rule 4. Rule 4's visible change here is the naming — the areas renamed by content and the door's content list and count — plus the lock treatment. `src/ollopa/ui/Door.tsx` belongs to the lesson-view builder, so this case does not reach into it to take the chevron away.
- **Meridian is on the Scale plan, so nothing on this stage is locked.** The lock branch is real code — before rule 4 a locked control is greyed with the vendor's own FAQ sentence, after it a real button with the plan and the price — but it only fires at Fathom, whose founder is on Starter. On this stage, step 4's visible change is the labels.
- **Step 1 keeps the settings shell**, because rule 1 is what collapses it. While the shell is still up, the strip carries two extra lines — Delete workspace, and the API limits with the delivery contract — for facts the category has no page for at all. At step 2 those lines go and the rows take their place in their own areas. The strip in the product has neither line.
- **Export all data stays at level two.** Spec 14 §8 records it moving beside Delete workspace as a rule-5 override, but the shipped page derives every level from the usage model and `plan.export` is a 2%-a-week item, so it sits behind the Plan door. Changing that would change the product, and a case may not do that. The gap is real and belongs to the spec or the usage model, not to this lesson.
- **Rule 2's demonstration is on the page, not inside the drawers.** The mailbox drawer's four tabs and the permission-profile editor's six accordions are described in the step text, but the panels in `panels.tsx` are already flat and this case did not add tabs to them in order to take them away again. What the viewer sees move at step 3 is the five areas whose rows come out of their tab strips.
- **Steps 2 and 3 look alike above the fold**, because the strip is tall and the areas that change at step 3 are further down. The moves are real and the delta finds them; the viewer has to scroll.
- **A row whose place is deleted leaves no ghost.** `applyDelta` puts a ghost where the thing was, and when the container itself is gone — a parody page at step 2, a tab strip at step 3 — there is nowhere to put one. The sidebar entries, which sat on the shell itself, do ghost: 36 of them at step 2.
- **The parody is trimmed to ollopA's boundary**, as spec 14 §5 says: no dialer, conversations, meetings or extension pages, because ollopA does not have those products. Everything else the memo records is kept, mistakes included.

## Files in this folder

- `case.ts`: the case's id, title, summary, the page node the stage renders (`P-settings`), the seat it signs in as (Meridian, admin) and the spec it is built from.
- `steps.ts`: the seven steps: rule, title, what moved, why, evidence quotes with the knowledge-base file each is in, and the doors to open on arrival so the change is visible.
- `scores.ts`: the nine rubric scores per step, 3 of 18 at the common version and 17 of 18 at the last.
- `screens/`: `step-0.png` and `step-last.png`, both at 1440.

The page itself lives in `src/ollopa/pages/settings/` and reads `useLesson()` (`src/learn/context.ts`): one component, one model, a layout per step. There is no separate "bad" and "good" implementation — the common version in `parody.tsx` deals the same `rowsFor(ctx)` rows into the shell the category ships. Step N is the page with N rules on; the last step renders the same DOM as the product, which is checked by comparing the 104 rows, the 13 doors and the 13 area headings on the stage against the same page at `#/ollopa/settings`.
